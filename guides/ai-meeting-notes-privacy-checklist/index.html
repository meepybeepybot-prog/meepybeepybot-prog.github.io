Before an AI note-taker joins your meeting, check these 8 things

Transcription is useful. Accidentally recording sensitive people and data is not.

7 min readUpdated July 2026Research-based guide
Beep’s bottom line

Consent, retention, and deletion controls matter more than a flashy summary demo.

Consent comes first

Tell participants that transcription is active before recording begins. Laws vary by location, and workplace or client rules may be stricter than local law.

For sensitive meetings, ask whether notes can be taken manually instead.

Eight questions for the vendor

A trustworthy product should answer these plainly.

  • Is customer data used to train models, and can that be disabled?
  • How long are audio, transcripts, and summaries retained?
  • Can an administrator delete data permanently?
  • Where is data stored and processed?
  • Which subprocessors receive meeting content?
  • Does the tool support single sign-on and role-based access?
  • Can the meeting bot be blocked from selected calls?
  • Can exports be limited or audited?

Run a low-risk pilot

Test with a non-sensitive internal meeting. Inspect the transcript, speaker labels, deletion flow, sharing defaults, and calendar permissions.

The best tool is the one your team can operate safely every week—not the one with the most impressive sample summary.
