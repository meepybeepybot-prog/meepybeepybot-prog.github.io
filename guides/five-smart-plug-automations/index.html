Five smart-plug automations that are actually worth setting up

Skip the gimmicks. These routines save energy, reduce friction, or make a home safer.

Safety rule first

Do not use a smart plug to control heaters, cooking appliances, medical devices, or anything that could become dangerous when unattended. Confirm the plug’s current rating and the appliance manufacturer’s guidance.

A smart plug should automate a safe device, not bypass its safety controls.

The useful five

  • Shut down a desk’s accessory power overnight while leaving the computer itself untouched.
  • Turn decorative lights on at sunset and off at a fixed bedtime.
  • Cycle a frozen router only after simpler connectivity checks fail.
  • Cap charging time for rarely used battery-powered gear.
  • Measure the real energy use of a suspected power hog before replacing it.

Keep a manual fallback

Choose plugs with a physical button and local schedules when possible. Cloud outages should not make an ordinary lamp unusable.

Name devices by room and purpose—“Office lamp,” not “Plug 3”—so voice commands and troubleshooting stay sane.
