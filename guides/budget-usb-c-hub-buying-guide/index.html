The USB-C hub checklist that prevents a bad buy

Seven specs matter more than the logo. Use this checklist before adding any hub to your cart.

Start with the laptop, not the hub

A USB-C connector does not guarantee video output, charging, or Thunderbolt speeds. Check your computer’s manual first. Look for DisplayPort Alt Mode, USB4, or Thunderbolt support beside the exact port you plan to use.

If the laptop cannot output video through that port, no passive hub can add the feature.

The seven-point checklist

Write down your minimum before comparing products.

  • Power delivery: enough passthrough wattage for your charger, with overhead for the hub.
  • Display: exact resolution and refresh rate, such as 4K at 60 Hz—not merely “4K.”
  • Data speed: 5 Gbps is fine for accessories; faster storage may benefit from 10 Gbps or USB4.
  • Port mix: count what stays connected every day, then add one spare.
  • Ethernet: choose gigabit only if you will actually plug into wired networking.
  • Card reader: confirm SD, microSD, or both, plus the speed class you need.
  • Cable design: a replaceable cable is easier to repair; a captive cable is simpler for travel.

Red flags

Avoid listings that omit refresh rate, power-delivery limits, or per-port speeds. A long feature list is not a substitute for a usable specification table.

For expensive laptops, favor brands with a clear warranty and accessible support over the absolute cheapest unknown listing.
